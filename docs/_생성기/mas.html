Draft section 37(1C) requires publication of each reserve assets attestation report "within such time as the Authority may specify". When MAS sets that period, I would ask it to take into account the time that an independent examination requires.

In four Circle reports (reference dates 30 January, 31 March, 30 April and 31 July 2026), the accountant signed 27, 29, 28 and 27 calendar days after the reference date, or 19 to 21 weekdays. Six reports from four other issuers (PYUSD and USDP 17 weekdays, USDG 18, RLUSD 19, GUSD 21 and USDT 23) widen the range across the ten reports to 17 to 23 weekdays. Weekdays are counted without adjusting for public holidays. I could not find publication dates, so the signature date is used as the earliest possible publication date.

One recent report took longer. Gemini's GUSD report for 30 June 2026 was signed 58 calendar days after the reference date; the file I hold for 31 July 2026 is marked as a draft, so I have not included it. MAS's 2023 Consultation Response (15 August 2023, paras 3.9 and 3.10) confirmed monthly independent attestation, with attestations published on the issuer's website and submitted to MAS no later than the end of the following month. Applying that deadline to 263 reports for eight tokens (January 2023 to August 2026, excluding the draft), I found that 259 were signed in time and 4 were not: USDC and EURC for January 2023 (signed 2 March), USDT for March 2023 (signed 9 May) and GUSD for June 2026 (signed 27 August). A fixed count of 31 calendar days would have classified 15 more reports as late, all with reference dates on the last business day before month-end. I therefore suggest that the deadline be expressed as a calendar date rather than a number of days. None of the ten sampled reports would have met a deadline of fewer than 17 weekdays.

A workable design, which I used in a draft disclosure standard I prepared for Korea, separates two documents: an issuer-prepared, management-signed monthly statement due within five business days, and an independently examined report due within 30 days after each quarter (or after each month, if monthly examination is required, with at least 25 business days allowed). Following the point above, I would now express the deadline for the examined report as a calendar date rather than a number of days.

Draft section 37(1A)(a) allows MAS to specify attestation standards. The engagements I read used AICPA attestation standards (Circle, the Paxos US tokens, RLUSD and GUSD), SSAE 3000 (USDG) and ISAE 3000 (Revised) (USDT), against criteria that were the AICPA 2025 Criteria for Stablecoin Reporting, NYDFS guidance or criteria set by the issuer. I suggest that the notice specify both the assurance level and the reporting criteria.

Reference times also differ: the 264 reports use 17:00 ET (109), 23:59 (101), end of day (39) or 17:00 SGT (15). I suggest that the notice specify the time of day and time zone and, because a month-end can fall on a weekend (31 January 2026 was a Saturday), state whether the reference date is the last calendar day or the preceding business day.
