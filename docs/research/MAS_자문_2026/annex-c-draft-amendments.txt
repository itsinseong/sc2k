                       PAYMENT SERVICES ACT 2019

An Act to provide for the licensing and regulation of payment service providers and
  MAS-regulated stablecoin issuers, the recognition and regulation of foreign
  stablecoin issuers as MAS-recognised stablecoin issuers and stablecoins issued by
  them, the oversight of payment systems and designated systemic stablecoins, and
  connected matters and to make consequential and related amendments to certain
  other Acts.

Holding out as the issuer of an MAS-regulated stablecoin, etc.
5A.—(1) A person must not hold the person out as the issuer of an “MAS-regulated
stablecoin” (or any of its derivatives in any language) unless the person is an MAS-
regulated stablecoin issuer.

   (2) A person must not hold any stablecoin out as an “MAS-regulated stablecoin” (or
any of its derivatives in any language), unless the stablecoin is an MAS-regulated
stablecoin.

  (3) To avoid doubt, a reference in this section to an “MAS-regulated stablecoin” (or
any of its derivatives in any language) does not include an MAS-recognised stablecoin.

   (4) A person that contravenes subsection (1) or (2) shall be guilty of an offence and
shall be liable on conviction —
       (a) in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding $125,000 or to
           imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 years or to both and, in the case
           of a continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $12,500 for every
           day or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction;
           or
       (b) in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $250,000 and, in the case of a
           continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $25,000 for every day
           or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction.




Application for licence to provide any payment service or hold out as an issuer of
an MAS-regulated stablecoin, etc.

6.—(1) A person that wishes to —
       (a) carry on a business of providing any type of payment service; or
       (b) hold out as an issuer of an MAS-regulated stablecoin or to hold any
            stablecoin that it issues out as an MAS-regulated stablecoin,
may apply to the Authority, in such form and manner as the Authority may require, for
the appropriate licence under this section.

  (2) The types of licences that may be applied for under subsection (1) are as follows:
       (a) a money-changing licence;
       (b) a standard payment institution licence;
       (c) a major payment institution licence.;
       (d) a stablecoin issuance licence.

   (3) A person must have in force a money-changing licence to be entitled to carry on
a business of providing a money-changing service, unless the person has in force a
standard payment institution licence or major payment institution licence that entitles
the person to carry on a business of providing that service.

  (4) A person must have in force a standard payment institution licence or a major
payment institution licence to be entitled to carry on a business of —
       (a) providing one of the following payment services:
           (i)       an account issuance service;

             (ii)      a domestic money transfer service;

             (iii)     a cross-border money transfer service;

             (iv)      a merchant acquisition service;
           (v)      an e-money issuance service;

           (vi)     a digital payment token service; or

      (b) providing 2 or more of the following payment services:
          (i)       an account issuance service;

           (ii)     a domestic money transfer service;

           (iii)    a cross-border money transfer service;

           (iv)     a merchant acquisition service;

           (v)      an e-money issuance service;

           (vi)     a digital payment token service;

           (vii)    a money-changing service.

   (5) Despite subsection (4), a licensee must have in force a major payment
institution licence if —
      (a) both of the following apply:
          (i)       the licensee carries on a business of providing one or more of
                    the following payment services:
                     (A)     an account issuance service (other than an e-money
                             account issuance service);
                     (B)     a domestic money transfer service;
                     (C)     a cross-border money transfer service;
                     (D)     a merchant acquisition service;
                     (E)     a digital payment token service;

           (ii)     the average, over a calendar year, of the total value of all
                    payment transactions that are accepted, processed or executed
                    by the licensee in one month exceeds —
                     (A)     $3 million (or its equivalent in a foreign currency), for
                             any one of those payment services; or
                     (B)     $6 million (or its equivalent in a foreign currency), for
                             2 or more of those payment services;

      (b) both of the following apply:
            (i)       the licensee carries on a business of providing an e-money
                      account issuance service;

            (ii)      the sum of the following amounts exceeds $5 million (or its
                      equivalent in a foreign currency):
                      (A)      the average, over a calendar year, of the total value in
                               one day of all e-money that is stored in any payment
                               account issued by the licensee to a person whom the
                               licensee has determined, according to such criteria as
                               the Authority may specify by written notice, to be
                               resident in Singapore;
                      (B)      the average, over a calendar year, of the total value in
                               one day of all e-money that is issued in Singapore, and
                               is stored in any payment account issued by the
                               licensee to any person whom the licensee has not
                               determined, according to such criteria as the
                               Authority may specify by written notice, to be
                               resident outside Singapore; or

       (c) both of the following apply:
           (i)       the licensee carries on a business of providing an e-money
                     issuance service;

            (ii)      the average, over a calendar year, of the total value in one day
                      of all specified e-money that is issued by the licensee exceeds
                      $5 million (or its equivalent in a foreign currency).

  (6) Where any licensee mentioned in subsection (5)(a)(i), (b)(i) or (c)(i) does not
have in force a major payment institution licence on the relevant date —
       (a) that licensee must, within the prescribed period, apply under section 7(1)
           to change that licensee’s licence to a major payment institution licence;

       (b) subsection (5) does not apply to that licensee —
           (i)       during the prescribed period mentioned in paragraph (a); and

                      (B)      is approved or refused by the Authority.

Variation or change of licence
   7.—(1) A licensee (other than an MAS-regulated stablecoin issuer) may apply to
the Authority, in the form and manner prescribed, for any of the following things:
       (a) a variation of the licensee’s standard payment institution licence or major
           payment institution licence by changing the types of payment services that
           the licence entitles the licensee to carry on a business of providing;
       (b) a change of the licensee’s money-changing licence to a standard payment
           institution licence or a major payment institution licence;
       (c) a change of the licensee’s standard payment institution licence to a
           money-changing licence or a major payment institution licence;
       (d) a change of the licensee’s major payment institution licence to a
           money-changing licence or a standard payment institution licence;.
       (e) a change of the licensee’s major payment institution licence to a stablecoin
           issuance licence.
   (2) The Authority may require an applicant to provide the Authority with such
information or documents in relation to the application as the Authority considers
necessary.
  (3) An application under subsection (1) must be accompanied by a non-refundable
application fee of the prescribed amount that is payable in such manner as the
Authority may specify by written notice.
  (4) The Authority may approve an application under subsection (1) subject to such
conditions or restrictions as the Authority thinks fit, or may refuse the application.
   (5) The Authority must not refuse an application under subsection (1) without
giving the applicant an opportunity to be heard.

Prohibition against solicitation
   9.—(1) A person (other than a licensee that is a payment service provider or an
exempt payment service provider), whether in Singapore or elsewhere, must not,
whether by that person or through any other person in Singapore or elsewhere, do any
of the following things:
       (a) offer to provide, or issue any advertisement containing any offer to
           provide, to the public in Singapore or any section of the public in
           Singapore, any type of payment service, whether in Singapore or
           elsewhere;
       (b) make an offer or invitation, or issue any advertisement containing any
           offer or invitation, to the public in Singapore or any section of the public
           in Singapore, to enter into any agreement relating to the provision by any
           person of any type of payment service, whether in Singapore or elsewhere.
   (2) A person that contravenes subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and shall
be liable on conviction —
       (a) in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding $125,000 or to
           imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 years or to both and, in the case
           of a continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $12,500 for every
           day or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction;
           or
       (b) in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $250,000 and, in the case of a
           continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $25,000 for every day
           or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction.
   (3) A person in Singapore (whether or not a licensee or an exempt payment service
provider) must not, on behalf of a person outside Singapore that is not a licensee and
is not an exempt payment service provider, do any of the following things:
       (a) offer to provide, or issue any advertisement containing any offer to
           provide, to the public in Singapore or any section of the public in
           Singapore, any type of payment service, whether in Singapore or
           elsewhere;
       (b) make an offer or invitation, or issue any advertisement containing any
           offer or invitation, to the public in Singapore or any section of the public
            in Singapore, to enter into any agreement relating to the provision by any
            person of any type of payment service, whether in Singapore or elsewhere.
   (4) A person that contravenes subsection (3) shall be guilty of an offence and shall
be liable on conviction —
       (a) in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding $125,000 or to
           imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 years or to both and, in the case
           of a continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $12,500 for every
           day or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction;
           or
       (b) in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $250,000 and, in the case of a
           continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $25,000 for every day
           or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction.
   (4A) A person (other than an MAS-regulated stablecoin issuer), whether in
Singapore or elsewhere, must not, through any other person in Singapore or elsewhere,
issue any advertisement to the public in Singapore or any section of the public in
Singapore containing any representation to the effect that the first-mentioned person is
an MAS-regulated stablecoin issuer or that any stablecoin issued by the first-
mentioned person is an MAS-regulated stablecoin.
   (4B) A person in Singapore must not, on behalf of a person that is not an MAS-
regulated stablecoin issuer, issue any advertisement to the public in Singapore or any
section of the public in Singapore containing any representation to the effect that the
second-mentioned person is an MAS-regulated stablecoin issuer or that any stablecoin
issued by the second-mentioned person is an MAS-regulated stablecoin.
  (4C) A person that contravenes subsection (4A) or (4B) shall be guilty of an offence
and shall be liable on conviction —

       (a) in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding $125,000 or to the
           imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 years or to both and, in the case
           of a continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $12,500 for every
           day or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction;
           or
       (b) in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $250,000 and, in the case of a
           continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $25,000 for every day
           or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction.
   (5) For the purposes of subsections (1), and (3), (4A) and (4B), in determining
whether an offer, invitation or advertisement is made or issued to the public in
Singapore or any section of the public in Singapore, a person must have regard to such
considerations as the Authority may prescribe.
  (6) A person whose business is to publish, or to arrange for the publication, of

Lapsing, surrender, revocation or suspension of licence
    11.—(1) A licence lapses —
        (a) if the licensee (being an entity) is wound up or otherwise dissolved,
            whether in Singapore or elsewhere;
        (b) if the licensee (being an individual) dies, becomes mentally incapacitated
            or is adjudicated a bankrupt; or
        (c) upon the occurrence of such other event as may be prescribed.
    (2) The Authority may revoke a licence if —
        (a) it appears to the Authority that any of the following persons is not a fit
            and proper person under the Guidelines on Fit and Proper Criteria:
                 (i) the licensee;

                (iii)   where the licensee is a partnership or limited liability
                        partnership, any partner of that partnership or limited liability
                        partnership;

                (iv)    where the licensee is a corporation, any 5% controller, 12%
                        controller, 20% controller or indirect controller of the licensee;

        (b) it appears to the Authority that either of the following is not satisfactory:
          (i)   the financial standing of the licensee;

         (ii)   the manner in which the licensee’s business is being conducted;

    this Act, or has failed, or continues to fail, to comply with any condition
    or restriction imposed, or any written notice issued, by the Authority
    under this Act;
(d) the licensee has failed to comply with any written notice issued by the
    Authority under the Monetary Authority of Singapore Act 1970;

   (5) Where a major payment institution has surrendered its licence, or the licence of

        (a) being satisfied that there is no outstanding claim by any payment service

        (b) receiving the closure certificate required under subsection (7).
   (6) Any security provided by a major payment institution under this section —
        (a) is not liable to be attached, sequestered or levied upon for or in respect of

        (a) all moneys received from payment service users who are customers of the

        (b) adequate provision has been made to meet any unforeseen liabilities in

   30.—(1) If the Authority is satisfied that a person has contravened section 28(1) or
(6) or has failed to comply with any condition imposed under section 28(3) or (4), or if
the Authority has served a written notice of objection under section 29, the Authority
may, by written notice —
      (a) direct the transfer or disposal of all or any of the shares in the licensee
          held by the person or any of the person’s associates (called in this section
          the specified shares) within such time or subject to such conditions as the
          Authority considers appropriate;
      (b) restrict the transfer or disposal of all or any of the specified shares; or
      (c) make such other direction as the Authority considers appropriate.
   (2) Where the Authority has issued any direction under subsection (1)(a) or

      (a) no voting rights may be exercised in respect of the specified shares, unless

                                  PART 2A
                         DESIGNATION OF STABLECOIN

Division 1 – Information gathering powers over the issuer and participants in respect
                                   of a stablecoin


Provision of information to Authority
   40A.—(1) The Authority may, by notice in the form and manner prescribed, require
any of the following persons to provide to the Authority, within a reasonable period
specified in the notice, such information relating to a stablecoin that the public or any
section of the public may purchase, as the Authority may require:
       (a) the issuer of the stablecoin;
       (b) any participant in respect of the stablecoin.
   (2) Without limiting subsection (1), the Authority may in a notice issued under that
subsection require any person mentioned in subsection (1a) or (1b) to provide, whether
in the form of a return to be provided on a periodic basis or otherwise —
       (a) information relating to the issuance of the stablecoin including but not
           limited to the volume of stablecoins issued and the market value of all
           stablecoins in circulation;
       (b) information relating to the provision of a digital payment token service in
           respect of the stablecoin;
       (c) information relating to the person’s business; and
       (d) such other information as the Authority may require for the purposes of
           this Part.
   (3) Subject to subsection (5), any person to whom a notice is issued under subsection
(1) must comply with the notice.

   (4) A person that contravenes subsection (3) shall be guilty of an offence and shall
be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $100,000 or to imprisonment for a term
not exceeding 2 years or to both and, in the case of a continuing offence, to a further
fine not exceeding $10,000 for every day or part of a day during which the offence
continues after conviction.

   (5) A person to whom a notice is issued under subsection (1) is not obliged to
disclose any information that the person is prohibited by any written law from
disclosing.




                       Division 2 – Designation of stablecoins
Power of Authority to designate stablecoin as a designated systemic stablecoin

   40B.—(1) The Authority may, by order published in the Gazette, designate a
stablecoin as a designated systemic stablecoin if the Authority is satisfied that any of
the following considerations applies:
      (a) any loss of value stability of the stablecoin (including where the value of
          the stablecoin deviates materially or persistently from the reference asset
          of the stablecoin), or a disruption to the issuance, redemption, transfer,
          settlement or continued operation of the stablecoin could —
               (i)    trigger or cause systemic disruption to the financial system of
                      Singapore, or;
               (ii)   affect the stability of, or public confidence in the financial
                      system of Singapore; or;
      (b) the designation is otherwise in the interests of the public or a section of
          the public.
  (2) Any order made under subsection (1) —
      (a) must identify the stablecoin and the issuer of the stablecoin; and
      (b) continues to have effect until the order is withdrawn by the Authority.
   (3) The issuer or any participant in respect of a stablecoin which is aggrieved by a
decision of the Authority to designate the stablecoin as a designated systemic
stablecoin may, within 30 days after the order is published in the Gazette, appeal in
writing to the Minister whose decision shall be final.
  (4) Despite the lodging of an appeal under subsection (3), the designation by the
Authority under this section continues to have effect pending the decision of the
Minister.
   (5) The Minister may, when deciding an appeal under subsection (3), direct the
Authority to withdraw the designation of the stablecoin as a designated systemic
stablecoin, and the direction takes effect from the date of the Minister’s decision.


Prohibition against holding out as the issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin
   40C.—(1) A person must not hold the person out as the issuer of a designated
systemic stablecoin unless the stablecoin has been designated by the Authority under
section 40B.
   (2) A person that contravenes subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and shall
be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $250,000 or to imprisonment for a term
not exceeding 3 years or to both, and in the case of a continuing offence, to a further
fine not exceeding $25,000 for every day or part of a day during which the offence
continues after conviction.


Withdrawal of designation of designated systemic stablecoin
   40D.—(1) The Authority may, by order in the Gazette, withdraw the designation of
any designated systemic stablecoin at any time if the Authority is of the opinion that
the applicable consideration in section 40B(1) is no longer valid or satisfied.
   (2) The Authority must not withdraw the designation of any designated systemic
stablecoin without giving the issuer and the participants in respect of the designated
systemic stablecoin an opportunity to be heard.




      Division 3 – Obligations of the issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin


Obligations of the issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin in relation to reserve
assets and redemption
  40E.—(1) The issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin must —
     (a) maintain reserve assets in relation to the designated systemic stablecoin that
         are not less than the par value of the designated systemic stablecoins in
         circulation;
     (b) provide each holder of any designated systemic stablecoin issued by the
         issuer the right to redeem the designated systemic stablecoin on a par value
         basis, for the equivalent value determined by reference to the reference asset
         of the designated systemic stablecoin, upon the issuer’s receipt of a
         redemption request from the holder; and
     (c) fulfil every redemption request within such time from the issuer’s receipt of
         the request as may be prescribed by the Authority.
  (2) The issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin must satisfy the requirements
prescribed by regulations made under section 103(1) in respect of —
     (a) any matter relating to the reserve assets mentioned in subsection (1)(a),
         including —
             (i) the maintenance of such reserve assets including the composition
                   and valuation of such reserve assets;

              (ii)   the extent to which such reserve assets may be commingled with
                     other assets held by the issuer;

             (iii)   the safeguarding of such reserve assets in the event of the
                     insolvency of the issuer; and

     (b) the terms and conditions that relate to the redemption of a designated
         systemic stablecoin.
  (3) A person that contravenes subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and shall
be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $250,000 and, in the case of a
continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $25,000 for every day or part of a
day during which the offence continues after conviction.
Obligations of the issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin in relation to
provision of information

   40F. —(1) The Authority may, by written notice, require the issuer of a designated
systemic stablecoin, to provide to the Authority, with such period as the Authority
may specify in the notice, all such information relating to the issuer’s business of
issuing designated systemic stablecoins as the Authority may specify in the notice.

  (2) Without limiting subsection (1), the Authority may in a notice issued under that
subsection require the issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin to provide —
    (a)   information relating to the issuance of the designated systemic stablecoin
          including but not limited to the volume of designated systemic stablecoins
          issued and the market value of all designated systemic stablecoins in
          circulation; and
    (b)   such other information as the Authority may require for the purposes of this

  (3) Subject to subsection (5), any person to whom a notice is issued under

   (4) A person that contravenes subsection (3) shall be guilty of an offence and shall
be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $100,000 or to imprisonment for a term
not exceeding 2 years or to both and, in the case of a continuing offence, to a further
fine not exceeding $10,000 for every day or part of a day during which the offence
continues after conviction.

   (5) A person to whom a notice is issued under subsection (1) is not obliged to
disclose any information that the person is prohibited by any written law from

Obligations of the issuer of a designated systemic stablecoin in relation to audit
of reserve assets

   40G.—(1) The Authority may, by written notice, require the issuer of a designated
systemic stablecoin to appoint at its own expense, an auditor, to carry out an audit in

relation to reserve assets (such audit called for the purposes of this section, a “reserve
assets attestation”).

  (2) Without limiting subsection (1), the Authority may, in the notice under that
subsection, require —
    (a)   the reserve assets attestations to be carried out at such frequency and in
          accordance with such standards as the Authority may specify;
    (b)   the auditor to submit a report of every reserve assets attestations (each report
          called the “reserve assets attestation report”) to the Authority in such form
          as may be specified and within such time as the Authority may allow; and
    (c)   the issuer to publish every reserve assets attestation report on its website
          within such time as the Authority may specify.

   (3) A person that contravenes subsection (1) shall be guilty of an offence and shall
be liable on conviction to a fine not exceeding $100,000 or to imprisonment for a term
not exceeding 2 years or to both, and in the case of a continuing offence, to a further
fine not exceeding $10,000 for every day or part of a day during which the offence
continues after conviction.

Interpretation of this Part

  40H.—(1) In this part, unless the context otherwise requires —

   “foreign corporation” means any body corporate formed or incorporated or
      existing outside Singapore;

   “MAS-recognised stablecoin issuer” means a foreign corporation that is
     recognised by the Authority under section 40J(4)(a) as an MAS-recognised
     stablecoin issuer;

   “MAS-recognised stablecoin” means a stablecoin that is issued by an
     MAS-recognised stablecoin issuer, and recognised by the Authority under
     section 40J(4)(b) as an MAS-recognised stablecoin.


Holding out as the issuer of an MAS-recognised stablecoin, etc.

   40I.—(1) A person must not hold the person or any other person out as the issuer
of an “MAS-recognised stablecoin” (or any of its derivatives in any language) unless
the person is an MAS-recognised stablecoin issuer.

(2) A person must not hold any stablecoin out as an “MAS-recognised stablecoin” (or
any of its derivatives in any language) unless the stablecoin is an MAS-recognised
stablecoin.

  (3) A person that contravenes subsection (1) or (2) shall be guilty of an offence and
shall be liable on conviction —
     (a) in the case of an individual, to a fine not exceeding $125,000 or
         imprisonment for a term not exceeding 3 years or to both and, in the case
         of a continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $12,5000 for every
         day or part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction; or
     (b) in any other case, to a fine not exceeding $250,000 and, in the case of a
         continuing offence, to a further fine not exceeding $25,000 for every day or
         part of a day during which the offence continues after conviction.

  (4) To avoid doubt, a reference to “MAS-recognised stablecoin” (or any of its
derivatives in any language) does not include an MAS-regulated stablecoin.
