### Q4 — minimum proportion of reserve assets in cash and bank deposits

```
MAS notes that the UK and EU require thresholds in the range of 5% to 30% for non-systemic stablecoins (paras 3.6 to 3.7). A comparable rule already exists for a much larger pool of assets: the 2010 amendments to Rule 2a-7, which governs US money market funds, set daily and weekly liquid asset minimums of 25% and 50% (17 CFR 270.2a-7(d)(4)). The SEC's release explaining that rule stated that under the higher liquidity thresholds it adopted, "approximately 90 percent of retail and institutional funds would have been able to satisfy the level of redemption demands during individual days as well as the week of greatest redemption pressure in the fall of 2008" (Release No. IC-29132, 75 FR 10060, 4 March 2010). I raise this only as a reference point for how a liquidity floor has been calibrated for a different pool of redemption-facing assets, not as a claim that stablecoin redemption behaviour would match a 2008 money market fund run.

Japan regulates this differently and may be a useful comparator. Under the Cabinet Office Ordinance on Electronic Payment Instruments Exchange Service Providers (Cabinet Office Ordinance No. 48 of 2023), Article 3, paragraph 1, as amended, made under Article 2(9) of the Act on Settlement of Funds (Act No. 59 of 2009, as amended by Act No. 66 of 2025), at least 50% of the reserve assets backing a yen-denominated trust-type stablecoin must be held as demand deposits, with the remainder held in short-term government or qualifying foreign bonds. This amendment took effect on 1 June 2026 (Financial Services Agency of Japan, announcement of 22 May 2026, https://www.fsa.go.jp/news/r7/sonota/20260522/20260522.html).

In the reports I examined, the share of reserves held as cash at banks varied widely between issuers and tokens:

- Circle, USDC (n=4): 13.82%, 13.95%, 14.57% and 15.56%, a mean of 14.47%. This is the share held outside the government money market fund; in one month it also includes directly held Treasuries, so it is an upper bound for cash. The remainder was held in a government money market fund invested in short-dated US Treasuries and overnight repo.
- Paxos, PYUSD (n=1, 31 Jul 2026): 3.52% cash at banks, 81.68% overnight reverse repurchase agreements, 14.80% US Treasuries.
- Paxos, USDG (n=1): 3.04% cash at banks, 56.96% government money market funds, 40.00% US Treasuries.
- Paxos, USDP (n=1): 77.98% cash at banks, 22.02% reverse repurchase agreements. This token is much smaller (about USD 32 million in reserves).

Two observations follow.

1. The observed cash share ranges from about 3% to 78% across these seven reports, and whether an issuer meets a given floor depends mainly on which assets count towards it. If only bank deposits count, a 15% floor would not be met by PYUSD or USDG, nor by USDC in three of the four months. If overnight repo or government money market fund shares also count, the same reports would clear a much higher floor. I would therefore ask MAS to define the eligible asset categories for the threshold before, or together with, setting the number. My sample is too small to support a specific percentage.

2. Whatever threshold is chosen, the disclosure regime under section 21C should require the issuer to publish the cash and deposit share on every reference date, to four decimal places of the coverage ratio and with the asset categories defined in the notice, so that compliance can be verified from the published report rather than only from supervisory returns. In the reports I examined, the cash share had to be reconstructed from a category labelled "other reserve assets", which in one month mixed cash with directly held Treasuries. A prescribed category list would remove that ambiguity.
```

### Q23 — reserve assets held across multiple issuing entities

```
I support the proposal that the amount of reserve assets held by each issuing entity be reported monthly. I suggest that the same information be included in the public disclosure under section 21C, not only in submissions to MAS. The rationale MAS gives in para 5.7, that bifurcation of the reserve pool across entities creates a risk that one entity cannot meet redemptions, applies equally to any structure in which the reserve pool is split across several custodians or several affiliated entities within one jurisdiction. Holders and market participants can only price that risk if the split is public.
```

### Q29 — banks issuing through a separate non-bank entity

```
I support the separate-entity requirement. Para 6.3 explicitly contemplates that the reserve pool may consist of cash deposited with the parent bank. This creates a concentration in which the asset backing the stablecoin is a claim on the same banking group that controls the issuer. In a stylised balance-sheet scenario, Coste finds that "converting retail deposits into stablecoin issuers' deposits weakens a bank's liquidity coverage ratio (LCR), turning a retail deposit into a wholesale deposit, even when these funds are reinvested in high-quality liquid assets" (European Central Bank Occasional Paper No. 353, 2024). I suggest two disclosure items under section 21C for such issuers:

1. the name of each custodian or deposit-taking institution holding reserve assets, with the amount held at each, on every reference date; and

2. the share of reserve assets held with the parent bank or any related corporation, so that the degree of intra-group concentration is visible.

This is the structure under discussion in Korea, where the central bank has proposed that won stablecoins be issued by bank-led consortia. In that context, the per-custodian breakdown is a direct way to see whether reserves are concentrated in one member bank. I note that voluntary disclosure of the custodian bank by name already exists in practice: two Korean digital asset exchanges publish the name of the bank holding customer won deposits in their periodic attestation reports without any legal obligation to do so, which suggests the cost of this disclosure item is low.
```

### Q30 — other comments: attestation timing and reference date rule

```
Section 37(1C) requires publication of each reserve assets attestation report "within such time as the Authority may specify". When MAS sets that period, I would ask it to take into account the observed time needed for independent examination. In the four Circle reports I examined (reference dates 30 Jan, 31 Mar, 30 Apr and 31 Jul 2026), the interval from the reference date to the accountant's signature was 27, 29, 28 and 27 calendar days, or 19 to 21 business days (n=4). To check whether this was specific to one issuer, I also read the most recent report from four other issuers: Paxos's joint PYUSD/USDP report, 17 weekdays [표지 확인 필요]; Paxos's USDG report, 18 weekdays [표지 확인 필요]; Tether's USDT report, 23 weekdays [표지 확인 필요]; Ripple's RLUSD report, 19 weekdays [표지 확인 필요]; and Gemini's GUSD report, 21 weekdays [표지 확인 필요]. For these five reports I counted weekdays without adjusting for public holidays, so the business-day figures would be equal or slightly lower. Across these five reports and the Circle sample, the interval ranged from 17 to 23 days on these counts. A publication deadline shorter than about three business weeks for an independently examined report would not be met by any issuer in this small sample.

A workable design, which I have adopted in my own draft standard, separates two documents: an issuer-prepared, management-signed monthly statement due within five business days of the reference date, and an independently examined report due within 30 days after the end of each quarter (or after each month, if monthly examination is required, with a deadline of not less than 25 business days).

On reference dates: one of the four Circle reports used 30 January rather than the month-end because 31 January 2026 fell on a Saturday. A rule of "last calendar day of the month" alone cannot be followed in every month; I would suggest "last calendar day, or the preceding business day where the last day is not a business day", stated in the notice under section 21C.
```

## 4. 9/16 초안에서 바뀐 요점

1. **Q4에 SEC 2010 규칙(90% 커버) 원문 인용과 일본 50% 규정을 새로 넣었다.** 일본은 시행일까지 확인했다(아래 5절).
2. **"the largest issuer in the market"(최대 발행사) 표현 세 곳을 지웠다.** 발행 규모 1위는 Tether이고 Circle/USDC는 그중 하나일 뿐이라 원래 표현이 틀렸다. "one of the larger fiat stablecoin issuers", "this issuer", "any issuer in this small sample"로 바꿨다.
3. **Q30에 발행사 5곳(Paxos, Tether, Ripple, Gemini)의 서명 소요일을 추가했다.** Circle 4개월 표본(19~21영업일)이 우연이 아니라는 근거가 늘었지만, 다섯 수치는 PDF 자동 추출값이라 제출 전 표지 확인이 필요하다.
4. **Q29에 학술 근거 한 줄(Coste, ECB Occasional Paper 353, 2024)을 넣었다.** 모은행 집중이 은행 유동성비율을 약화시킨다는 공신력 있는 근거다.
5. **절대·최상급 표현을 걷어냈다.** "guaranteeing"(보장), "the only way"(유일한 방법)를 지우고 표본 크기("n=4", "five reports", "this small sample")를 문장마다 명시했다.
6. GENIUS법, FCA, MiCA를 축자 인용한 문장은 이 답변의 FormSG 영문 블록 안에는 원래 없었다(1절과 2절의 개요표에만 있고 개요표는 이번 파일에 다시 신지 않았다). 9/21 검증대장에서 정정된 세 건(GENIUS §4(a)(1)(A)(ii), FCA ODDR 문장, MiCA 조문 번호)은 노션 PRD와 공시안 문서에 반영해야 하는 항목으로, 이 자문 답변 파일 밖의 작업이다.
